# Skill routing: pseudo-queries per skill, djev rerank over bodies (#1490, 2026-09-25)

**Verdict.** Arm (a), Skill2Query pseudo-queries inside the lexical scorer:
**landed off** (`skills.pseudo_queries.enabled`, code default false, absent from
config.yaml). The first generation looked like a clear win, and a second,
independent generation did not reproduce it. Arm (b), djev listwise rerank of the
lexical top-16 over skill bodies: **rejected**. Its gain is fragile, it adds
~450 ms to every prefetch turn on a MAX_SEQS=1 engine the vault recall already
uses, and it does not stack on arm (a). The eval harness keeps both arms. The
one-line-descriptions arm was decided in #1501 / `72f678d5` and is not re-run
here. Everything below is measured with the descriptions index on, which is the
live config.

## Set-up

- **Gold set.** `eval/skill_match_queries.yaml` has 75 records: 51 labelled with
  expected skills and 24 expected-empty (the #311 precision control). 189 live
  skills. The lexical baseline today is **recall@5 0.6961**, not the 0.745 the
  item quotes. The recorded baseline (`skill_match_baseline.json`, 2026-09-24)
  also says 0.6961; the skill inventory has moved since the 0.745 number.
- **Metrics.** recall@5, hit@1 and MRR@5 over the 51 labelled turns, plus
  `named` (the matcher names a skill on an expected-empty turn) over the 24
  empty ones. **hit@1 is the one that matters for injection**: the first
  injectable skill gets its full body, and the second gets a 200-char excerpt
  only at a score of 4.0 or more. CIs are paired bootstraps from
  `eval/stats.py` (10k resamples, seed fixed), arm minus lexical, with n
  printed beside each.
- **Pseudo-queries.** `scripts/skill_pseudo_queries.py` asks for 8 per skill,
  generated by the primary (thinking off, temperature 0.7, run under the shared
  primary lock). **Leakage guard:** the prompt holds the skill's name,
  description, tags and the first 6,000 chars of its body, and nothing else. No
  eval turn and no session text is ever sent (a test pins this). I also audited
  generated queries against eval turns by the max token-Jaccard between each
  turn and every pseudo-query. The median is 0.20. The top overlap was 0.75:
  "full systems check please" vs "Run a full system check on everything", and
  that phrase comes from the `system-health-check` skill's own text. No other
  pair went above 0.5. Caches live in `~/lloyd-data/eval/1490/`:
  `pseudo_queries.json` is seed 1490, `pseudo_queries_seed2.json` is seed 2.
- **Scoring.** A query token found in a skill's pseudo-queries and in none of
  its name, description or tag tokens is a metadata hit worth `weight` points.
  In `union` mode it counts against the union of all 8 queries; in `max` mode
  against the best single query. The existing weights (name 3, description 2,
  tag 1.5, capped body 0.3) and the 3.0 injection gate are untouched.

## Gold-label audit

I read all 51 labelled turns against the lexical top-6 and the skill
descriptions. Three labels are disputable:

- `smq029` names `memory-path-scoping` for a turn that is really "move a
  folder".
- `smq043` asks a question *about* opencode; the file's own rule makes that
  expected-empty.
- `smq060` names `restart-lloyd` for a turn asking what changed.

Every headline is also given with these three excluded (n=48).

## Arm (a): pseudo-queries

Generation 1 (seed 1490), all weights run in one pass:

| arm | recall@5 | hit@1 | MRR@5 | empty named |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lexical | 0.6961 | 0.4902 | 0.5889 | 15/24 |
| union w1.5 | 0.7745 | 0.6078 | 0.6745 | 16/24 |
| union w2 | 0.7745 | 0.6471 | 0.6977 | 16/24 |
| union w3 (the item's "name weight") | 0.7712 | 0.6275 | 0.6925 | **22/24** |
| max w1.5 / w2 | 0.7647 | 0.5882 | 0.658 | 15/24 |

On seed 1490, union w2 minus lexical (n=51):

- hit@1: **+0.157** [+0.059, +0.255], p=0.001.
- MRR: +0.109 [+0.049, +0.176].
- recall: +0.078 [0.000, +0.167].
- empty named: +0.042 [0, +0.125] (n=24).

With the three disputed labels excluded, hit@1 is still +0.146 [+0.063, +0.250]
(n=48).

The item's pre-declared weight, 3.0 (name weight), fails the precision control:
expected-empty turns that name a skill go from 15 to 22 of 24, +0.29
[+0.13, +0.50]. Weights 1.5 and 2 do not have that problem.

**Replication.** I regenerated every skill's queries with seed 2 and changed
nothing else:

| arm, seed 2 | recall@5 | hit@1 | hit@1 diff vs lexical (n=51) |
|---|---|---|---|
| union w1.5 | 0.7353 | 0.5686 | +0.078 [-0.039, +0.196], p=0.26 |
| union w2 | 0.7353 | 0.5294 | +0.039 [-0.078, +0.157], p=0.63 |
| union w3 | 0.7647 | 0.4902 | 0.000, with empty named 21/24 |

As an exploratory check, the union of both generations (16 queries a skill) at
w2 gives hit@1 +0.078 [-0.059, +0.216] and recall +0.075 [0.000, +0.160]; at
w1.5 its MRR is +0.068 [+0.001, +0.136].

What goes into the prompt barely moves. I counted turns where an expected skill
is in the injection plan at all (body or excerpt), using
`prefetch._skill_injection_plan`:

- lexical 33/51, pq 34/51, on both generations;
- expected-empty turns that inject anything: 15 → 16 of 24.

The seed-1 hit@1 gain therefore came from promoting an expected skill from the
excerpt slot to the body slot, not from finding new ones.

`eval/run_skill_activation_eval.py` (#711 corpus, 5 covered skills: 30 positives,
350 negatives), lexical vs pq w2:

- lexical: 19/30 triggers, 5/350 false triggers;
- seed 1490: 18/30 triggers, 7/350 false triggers;
- seed 2: 19/30 triggers, 7/350 false triggers.

Cost:

- `_search_skills` plus the plan goes from p50 0.24 ms to 0.63 ms and p90
  0.38 ms to 1.08 ms.
- Generation takes ~77 s for 189 skills on an idle primary, and 17 min on a
  loaded one.

**Reading.** The direction is positive on both generations (hit@1 +0.04 to
+0.16, recall +0.04 to +0.08). The effect size depends on which 8 queries the
sampler happened to write, and only one of the two generations clears the
interval. The weight was also picked after seeing generation 1. Activation is
flat to slightly worse. This does not meet the bar for a flag flip. It stays
default-off, cheap to re-measure, and waiting for a larger gold set (P0
LloydMemEval, or a regrown `skill_match_queries.yaml`).

## Arm (b): djev listwise rerank over skill bodies

The rerank takes the lexical injectable set (≤16), passes djev
`name: description\nbody` clipped per candidate, and reorders. The gate, and so
the expected-empty control, are unchanged.

| arm | recall@5 | hit@1 | hit@1 diff (n=51) | djev latency p50 / p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 chars | 0.7059 | 0.6078 | +0.118 [-0.039, +0.255], p=0.16 | 617 / 998 ms |
| 160 chars | 0.7451 | 0.6275 | +0.137 [+0.020, +0.255], p=0.03 | 427 / 501 ms |
| 160 chars, repeat | identical | identical | identical | 456 / 517 ms |
| 160 chars on pq w2 (seed 1490) | 0.8007 | 0.5882 | +0.098 [-0.059, +0.255] | 483 / 515 ms |

- djev answered all 56 calls with 0 failures.
- 160 chars is the only djev setting that clears the interval, and with the
  three disputed labels excluded its lower bound is 0.000 (+0.125 [0.000,
  +0.250], n=48).
- It adds ~0.45 s to every turn's prefetch on an engine with MAX_SEQS=1 that
  every vault recall already queues on.
- It does not stack: on top of pq it lowers hit@1 relative to pq alone.

**Rejected.** A slower change needs a gain clear of the interval, and this one
is not robustly clear. It is not wired into `prefetch`.

## What landed

- `agent_mcp/skills.py`:
  - `skills.pseudo_queries` settings (`enabled`, `weight` default 3.0, `mode`
    union/max, `path`);
  - `skill_text_hash`, so a cached entry for an edited skill is ignored, never
    trusted;
  - an mtime-keyed cache load, and a per-skill memo;
  - `_score_skill(..., pseudo_queries=)`.

  Off is byte-for-byte the old scorer.
- `prefetch._search_skills` reads the index once per turn.
- `app.paths.SKILL_PSEUDO_QUERIES_PATH` = `${LLOYD_DATA}/_pipeline/skill-pseudo-queries.json`
  (the production cache location, not generated).
- `scripts/skill_pseudo_queries.py`: the generator. It is incremental by hash,
  takes `--seed`, and needs the primary lock.
- `eval/run_skill_match_eval.py`:
  - new metrics: hit@1, MRR@5;
  - new flags: `--pseudo-queries/--pq-weight/--pq-mode` and
    `--rerank djev --rerank-k/--rerank-chars/--rerank-pool`;
  - each arm is scored against lexical with a paired bootstrap per metric.
- `tests/test_skill_pseudo_queries.py`.

To re-measure:

    .venvs/lloyd/bin/python eval/run_skill_match_eval.py \
        --pseudo-queries ~/lloyd-data/eval/1490/pseudo_queries_seed2.json --pq-weight 1.5,2
    .venvs/lloyd/bin/python eval/run_skill_match_eval.py --rerank djev --rerank-chars 160

Turning it on later needs three things:

- a production cache at the paths constant;
- a scheduled regeneration, because skills are rewritten nightly and a stale
  hash silently drops that skill's queries;
- a replicated gain.

`run_prefetch_cost_eval.py` (live turns, `right_skill_reached`) was not run.
Neither arm changed which expected skills reach the prompt at all (33 → 34 of
51), so the model-turn eval could at most price body versus excerpt. That is
not worth a 51×N turn run on a contended primary for an arm that failed to
replicate.
