## A verifier bug the first trial found

The zero-item control's first trial answered correctly ("zero findings") and
then explained itself in bullets, one naming `74-kg-mention-classifier.md`. The
item regex accepted *any* bullet containing a task file name, so that commentary
counted as a submission and the correct answer scored 0. The item regexes of
#014/#016/#017 now require the bullet to **lead** with the answer token; the
trials below were re-scored offline with the fixed regexes (objective half only,
deterministic; rubric scores kept). Two rows changed (#017 all/0 0→1, #016 one/1
P 0.57→1.0). Pinned by `test_a_commentary_bullet_that_names_a_file_is_not_a_finding`.
This is exactly the "verifier bugs are silent" risk: without a trial read by
hand it would have stood.

## Measurement

`eval/run_find_all_arms.py`, `bench_runner_sdk` harness path (sandboxed
`<date>_bench_<hex>` sessions; `/state.tool_sandbox` enforced), primary,
`max_agent_turns` 12 (the bench default), judge on the primary.
Planned n = 3 per (task, arm); the run hit its 45-min lock budget with the
engine shared by three other evals, so **n = 2**. One trial (#014 all/1) timed
out at 600 s and is not rankable. Raw rows: `find-all-2026-09-24/trials.jsonl`,
re-scored `trials_rescored.jsonl`.

**The 12-iteration cap decides more than the phrasing does.** Three of eight
`all` trials (016 twice, 015 once) used all 12 iterations and ended with no
text: scored 0/0. 016 never finished a "find all" at that budget, while "find
one" (which may stop early) finished both.

**Phrasing arm.** On #014 the gap is clean: recall 1.00 (the finished "all"
trial, 15/15) against 0.067 in both "one" trials (1/15). On #016 it is
confounded by the cap (0 vs 0.63). A measurable recall gap on one task, not the
two the item's acceptance asks for, at n = 2.

**Spam arm** (each `all` reply re-scored with three hand-checked wrong extras).
Of the 8 pairs, 4 carry an answer to pad (the rest are the 3 empty replies and
the timeout). On all 4 the objective P×R fell (mean −0.33 over all 8) and the
composite fell. The rubric did not follow: it rose on 2 of the 4 (015: 0.85 →
0.88; 017: 0 → 0.25) and gave **1.0 to a reply that was padding only** (016, the
empty replies plus three wrong bullets). Precision is the only term here that
penalises spam.

**Judge independence** (rubric vs P×R on the 11 rankable `all`/`one` trials):
Pearson 0.30; they disagree at the 0.5 line on **4 of 11 (36%, Wilson 95%
15–65%)**, above the item's 20% bar — recorded as the LLM-judge error rate on
this corpus. The sharpest case: #017 all/1 said, correctly, that nothing
qualifies (P = R = 1) and the rubric gave it 0.0.

## Decisions

- **Keep P×R in the composite** (as `b2f0bc24` landed it: a `find_all` check's
  credit is P×R inside the objective half) **and keep P/R/F1 reported beside
  it.** The spam arm is the reason: it is the only term that moved the right way.
  The old 13 tasks score exactly as before, so the promotion FP rate measured at
  0.5 is not invalidated for them; but the bench is now 17 tasks
  (granularity 0.059), so `promotion_fp_rate.py` should be re-run before
  autoresearch is re-armed (it is off pending #876).
- **Do not weight the rubric on set tasks yet**: a 36% disagreement says the
  rubric half adds noise here, but changing the weighting per task is a judge
  change to measure on its own.
