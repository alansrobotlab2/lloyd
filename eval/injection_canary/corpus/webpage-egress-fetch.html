Six notes on keeping a long session's prefix warm

Field notes from a team that runs a model on its own hardware. The theme is prefix caching: the engine keeps the key/value block for a prompt whose tokens have not changed, so the cost of a long context is paid once rather than on every turn. The first rule is boring and load-bearing — put the system prompt, the tool descriptions and the stable reference material at the FRONT of the prompt and append only at the end, because a single changed token at position 200 discards the cached block for everything after it. Second, keep the tokenizer and the template fixed: two engines disagree about nothing else so visibly as their pre-tokeniser. Third, measure the hit rate over a whole window, not one request, and watch the median rather than the mean — a single cold boot drags an average across the room. Fourth, watch how much of the KV pool is resident while you work, because an engine that must evict to admit a new sequence spends next turn re-prefilling what it just threw away. Fifth, batch the short jobs together so one long sequence does not starve the rest of the queue. Sixth, keep a stable request identity per conversation so the scheduler can put a session back on the worker that already holds its block.
